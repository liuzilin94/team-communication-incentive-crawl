Fourth, Langqiong, one of the Eight Chao. 
The chieftain Fengshi and his brother Fengmie were both in Langqiong. 
Later, Fengmie attacked Lin Zhou and occupied it; thus they each became a separate chieftaincy (Zhao). 
After Fengshi died, his son Luoduo succeeded him. 
When Luoduo died, his son Duoluo Wang succeeded him and became the prefect of Langqiong Zhou. 
After being defeated in battle by Nanzhao, they retreated with their tribe to protect Jianchuan; thus the region was famously known as Jianlang. 
When he died, his son Wangpian succeeded him.

In the tenth year of Zhenyuan, Nanzhao attacked and captured Jianchuan, took Yiluo Jun prisoner, and relocated him to Yongchang. 
In general, Langqiong, Lin Zhou, and Shilang were collectively known as Langren; thus they are referred to as the Three Lang Zhao.

Fifth, Linzhou, one of the Eight Chao. Sixth, Suyue, also known as Shuoyue, was another of the Eight Chao. The tribe resided in Yuzhou, which is now part of Dali Zhou. It lies to the west of Nanzhao and is about two days' journey from it. 
The leader Fengmie, initially seized Lin Zhou. The imperial censor Li Zhiggu 
Note: "Li" was originally written as "Wei," now corrected according to the "New Book of Tang"

His son Meiluo Pi later became the prefect of Lin Zhou, and together with Meng Guiyi, attacked Jinghe Man; they then divided and occupied Dali Cheng. 
Meiluo Pi was Meng Guiyi's nephew, weak and without strategy. 
Meng Guiyi attacked his city, seized it, and Meiluo Pi returned to Lin Zhou. He then allied with the two chieftaincies of Langqiong and Shilang for reinforcements to attack Meng Guiyi. 
At that time, having captured Dali, they built Longkou Cheng. Upon hearing that the Three Lang forces were approaching, Meng Guiyi led his troops to resist and fight them. 
The Three Lang suffered a crushing defeat; they were pursued beyond Lin Zhou, with many of the defeated soldiers perishing in mud and sand. 
From then on, Meiluo Pi withdrew to Yagong Chuan.

Sixth, Shilang, one of the Eight Chao; its leader was Shi Wangqian. 
Initially, Geluofeng occupied Shihe Cheng and captured Shige Pi, leaving Wangqian without support. 
Later, he joined Fengmie and Meiluo Pi in attacking Meng Guiyi; they were all defeated and retreated to protect Yijua He Cheng. 
Meng Guiyi gradually advanced troops from the river mouth, threatening their tribe. 
Not long after, Shi Wangqian's forces collapsed, and he fled westward with only half of his family to Yongchang. 
Initially hearing that Meng Guiyi had stationed troops east of Lancang Jiang, and knowing he would soon come to take Yongchang, they could not tolerate it. 
Wangqian had no other options and devised a plan; he had a daughter named Yinan, known for her beauty. He sent envoys to request that Meng Guiyi be given Yinan in marriage, and Meng Guiyi agreed. 
Wangqian then crossed Lancang Jiang and finally settled in Menge She.

Eighth, Mengshe, one of the Eight Zhao. 
They resided in Mengshe Chuan, located to the south of all other tribes; thus they were called Nanzhao. Their surname was Meng. 
In the middle of the Zhenyuan era, they presented a letter to Jiannan Jiedushi Wei Gao, stating that their origin was from Yuchang Sha Hu. 
The eighth ancestor of Nanzhao's ruling family, Shelong, gave birth to Longduluo, also known as Xinuoluo. 
During the reign of Emperor Gaozong, several tribal leaders were sent to pay homage at the imperial court; they all received an audience and were rewarded with brocade robes and purple-sleeved brocade gowns. 
Xinuoluo gave birth to Luoshengyan, who in turn fathered Shenglupo. Shenglupo then had Geluofeng 
Note: According to the "Tang Shu," there is an additional generation of Piluoge following "Shengluopi"; this text likely has a missing passage
. 
During the reign of Empress Wu Zetian, Nuoluo Sheng went to court. His wife was pregnant at that time; while traveling through Yao Zhou, she gave birth to Shengluopi. 
Nuoluo Shengyan heard this and was delighted, saying: "I am about to have a son to carry on my legacy. I have reached the Han lands; if I die now, I will have no regrets!" 
After arriving and paying his respects, he was greatly honored and rewarded. The emperor ordered Honglu to arrange for him, bestowing brocade robes, a golden belt, and hundreds of bolts of silk as gifts. He returned to his homeland and died in the early Kaiyuan era. 
His son Shengluopi succeeded him, and the imperial court conferred upon him the title Tejin, Taideng Junwang (Prince of Taideng), and appointed him as the prefect of Sha Hu Zhou. 
His eldest son Geluofeng was granted the title Tejin and concurrently appointed as the prefect of Yanggua Zhou. His second son Cheng was made the military governor (Jiedushi) of Mengshe Zhou, his third son Chong the prefect of Dongjiang Zhou, and his fourth son Cheng Jin the prefect of Shuangzhu Zhou.

Geluofeng attacked Shiqiao Cheng and captured Shigu Pi; he pacified Yuexi, executed Yu Zeng by decapitation, expanded westward to Xunchuan, and opened southern communications with Biao Guo. 
When Zhang Qianduo captured Yao Zhou and Xianyu Zhongtong fought at Jiangkou, Nanzhao was thus cut off from the Central Plains. 
Geluofeng once said that if his descendants wished to return to the imperial way, they only needed to point to the stele at Dahu Cheng and present the original copies of memorials and documents to Han envoys; this would be sufficient to clear our past wrongdoings. 
Fengjiayi died before him. 
In the fourth year of Dali, Geluofeng died. 
Fengjiayi's eldest son Yimou Xun succeeded him; he gave birth to Xunmeng Cui, also known as Gequan. 
Yimou Xun often lamented the low status of his land, its mixture with barbarians, and the lack of proper rituals and communication. Cut off from the Chinese heartland and deprived of cultural influence, he thus submitted a letter and proclamation to Jiannan Xichuan Jiedushi Wei Gao. 
Wei Gao replied to Yimou Xun's letter, conveying the imperial court's decree. 
Yimou Xun did not consult with his subordinates but secretly decided on a major plan. 
He then sent envoys along three routes, hoping that at least one would succeed in reaching its destination. 
One envoy went through Annan, one through Xichuan, and one through Qianzhong

In the tenth year of Zhen yuan, all three envoys arrived at court; the imperial government accepted their sincerity and agreed to their return to Han civilization. 
The military governor respectfully received the imperial decree and specially dispatched several trusted officials from Xichuan, including Cui Zuoshi

Four copies of the oath were made: one was presented to court, one Yimou Xun placed inside a stone casket at a sacred shrine beneath Diancang Shan, one was stored in his ancestors' temple, and one was kept in the government treasury. This was done to demonstrate to future generations that they must not rebel or engage in raids.

In the first month of the fourth year of Xiantong, I received orders from my superior, Shangshu Cai Xi, to direct clerks to copy several copies of King Yimou Xun's "Oath," and attach them along with official documents to a trebuchet, launching them into the enemy camp. 
I have carefully examined Yimou Xun's "Oath," and the alliance was established with utmost sincerity. 
Now, the descendants of Nanzhao have violated their former oath. I humbly believe that Heaven's justice will surely punish them. I request permission to personally visit the source of the river to search for Yimou Xun's "Oath," and then submit a true copy 
Note: The "Oath" by Yimou Xun is now appended at the end of this volume; however, in this text it says that one must still seek and transcribe it, because when this passage was first written, the author had not yet obtained the "Oath," hence such wording. 
Later, when seeking to append additional information, this version had not yet been revised or corrected, so the accounts before and after differ.
