The Xi Cuan were a branch of the Bai man. 
The Dong Cuan were a branch of the Wu man. 
During the Tianbao period, from Qujing Zhou in the northeast to Xuancheng in the southwest, villages and settlements could be seen from one another, with cattle and horses scattered across the fields. 
In Shicheng, Kunchuan, Qu'e, Jinning, Yuxian, Anning to Longhe Cheng, they were known as the Xi Cuan. 
In Qujing Zhou, Milu Chuan, Shengma Chuan, and as far south as Butou, they were known as the Dong Cuan; this was a name derived from their customs. 
Initially, Cuan Guiwang was the Dudu of Nanning Chuan, governing Shicheng. He attacked and killed Gai Cheng and his son Gai Qi
Note: The original text erroneously reads "Meng Ke" and "Meng Qi"; these have now been corrected according to the New Book of Tang
, thereby gaining control over Shengma Chuan. 
Guiwang's elder brother Mo ??? gave birth to Chongdao, who governed Qu'e Chuan and became the great ghost lord of both Cuan groups. 
Chongdao's younger brothers Rijin and Riyong were in Anning Cheng. 
When Zhangqiu Jianqiong opened the Butou Road, it was then that a city was built at Anning. 
The groups of man rebels stirred up unrest and captured and killed the envoy sent to build the city. 
Xuanzong dispatched envoys to order Yunnan Wang Meng Guiyi to campaign against them. 
Guiyi's army was stationed at Bo Zhou, when Guiwang and his brothers Chongdao, Cuan Yanzhang and more than ten others came to the military gate to bow and thank, requesting that their past matters be reported and cleared. 
Guiyi submitted a public memorial which was heard by the emperor; after going back and forth for twenty-five days, an imperial edict came down pardoning all of them. 
Not long afterwards, Chongdao killed Rijin, and secretly harmed Guiwang as well. 
Guiwang's wife Achacha was a daughter of the Wu man; she fled to her parents and raised troops, leading to a standoff, causing chaos among the Cuan chieftains. 
Achacha secretly sent envoys to Wumeng She Chuan to seek refuge, and Guiyi immediately submitted a memorial to report the matter. 
Achacha's son Shouou 
Note: "Shouou" is written as "Shouyu" in the New Book of Tang
, thus succeeded Guiwang as the Duke of Ning Zhou, and Guiyi still married his daughter to him. 
He also married one of his daughters to Chongdao's son Fuchao. 
Chongdao harbored inner resentment and regret, but outwardly showed peace; he still engaged in daily attacks with Shouou's mother and son. 
Achacha again petitioned Guiyi, who then raised an army to inquire about the crime. 
While stationed at Kunchuan for two nights, Qu'e Chuan collapsed and scattered, and Chongdao fled south to Lichuan. 
Guiyi captured all of their family members and supporters, and killed Fuchao while taking his daughter. 
Soon after, Chongdao was also killed, and thus the various Cuan groups became divided and weakened. 
After Guiyi died, his son Ge Luofeng was enthroned. Shouou and his wife returned to Hedui 
Note: "Hedui" originally written as "Ati," now corrected according to the "Tang Shu"
; from then on, they were cut off from Huanghua. 
Achacha declared herself as the tribal queen of the Wu man, followed an imperial envoy to the capital for court attendance, and received great favors and rewards. 
Ge Luofeng sent the Kun Chuan Cheng envoy Yang Muli to use troops and intimidate the Xi Cuan, relocating more than twenty thousand households to Yongchang Cheng. The Wu man were spared from relocation because they could not communicate in the language, so many dispersed into forests and valleys. 
After this, from Qujing Zhou, Shicheng, Shengma Chuan and Kunchuan to the south as far as Longhe, there was nothing but war and devastation. 
The descendants of Riyong are now established in Yongchang Cheng. 
Within the region, the Wu man tribes gradually revived; later they migrated to live in the former territory of the Xi Cuan and are now families intermarried with Nanzhao.

Since the eighth year of Dazhong, the Annan Duduhu arbitrarily dismissed the winter garrison troops at Linxi Yuan. The dongzhu Li Youdu and seven other chieftains were lured by the Nan man, re-established kinship ties, coming and going daily and monthly, gradually suffering invasions and outrages; this was due to the Duduhu's failure in his duty of recruitment and suppression, neglecting his responsibility for strategic planning. 
In the third month of spring in the third year of Xiantong, I received a personal order from Shangshu Cai Xi, the original envoy, secretly entrusting me to ride alone with no more than twenty strong runners and penetrate deeply into the camp of the rebel leader Zhu Daigu. 
On the eighth day of the third month, I entered deep within the enemy's encirclement. 
The Nan rebel general Yang Bingzhong, Da Qiang Yang Achu, and Yang Qiusheng were all Wu man rebels who came to meet me together with deceptive words. 
I, Xie Jiong, reported each of these matters one by one to Duduhu Wang Kuan. 
From then on, Kuan was not clear-minded and had no long-term planning. Upon receiving my documents, he gave no orders at all but arbitrarily sent the troops back, seeking only imperial commendation, which led to my original envoy Cai Xi being unjustly injured by arrows and stones, losing cities and fortresses. 
Investigating the cause of these events, it is none other than the fault of Wang Kuan! 

It is also said: Cai Xi led troops to replace Kuan, who had already been replaced. How could he arbitrarily send the army back and cause Xi's defeat? In the tenth year of Zhenyuan, he served as a general and built a city in Bonong Dong Chuan. 
In the tenth year of Zhen Yuan, as Shangshu Cibu Langzhong and concurrently Nei Geishi Ju Wenzhen and Liu Youyan entered Yunnan, carrying a ceremonial staff to bestow the title of Yunnan Wang upon Nan Zhao Yi Mouxun, serving as a buffer for the southwest.

The Nongdong man were descendants of the Bai man. 
Originally, they were a tribal group from Ya Zhou Nongdong Xian; their territory was formerly known as Bao Zhou. 
There had once been a tribal leader who served as Cishi, mistakenly assaulted and killed a Shihu officer; when the case was brought to trial in the prefectural city, he and his entire family were executed. 
The "Nanzhao Zhuan" states: "There was one who served as Cishi and mistakenly killed his Canjun, leading the clan to flee northward." 
According to the text, the character "卒" should be "率," and below "jiazhong," there should be the words "bei zou." This is likely an omission in the original. Afterwards, they dispersed along both sides of Moxie River, and all the regions including Jian Gong Chuan had them; other tribes did not leave.

During the Tianbao period, Ya Zhou Cishi Zhang Qiantuo defended the city and resisted battle, and was almost entirely captured or killed. 
In the tenth year of Zhenyuanyuan, Nanzhao Yimouxun captured and plundered Tubo's cities and towns, seized Nongdong Cheng, and moved it to Yongchang Cheng.

The Chang people were originally Han Chinese. 
Their tribal settlement was located north of Tie Qiao, but the exact time and month of their migration are unknown. 
Initially they wore Han clothing, but later gradually adopted some customs from the surrounding ethnic groups; to this day, they only wrap their heads with chaoxia (a kind of cloth), and otherwise have no differences. 
In the tenth year of Zhen yuan, Nanzhao Yi Mouxun led troops to attack and capture Tubo's Tieqiao Jiedu Cheng, capturing thousands of households of Chang people, who were all relocated to various regions in northeastern Yunnan. 
Now Tieqiao Cheng is occupied by the Nan man and a major general has been appointed as its city commander.

長褌蠻，本烏蠻之後，部落在劍川，屬浪詔。其本俗皆衣長褌曳地，更無衣服，惟牛羊皮。南詔既破，劍浪遂遷其部落，與施、順諸蠻居養給之。
The Changkuan man were descendants of the Wu Man, with their tribal settlement located in Jian Chuan, belonging to Langzhao. 
Their original custom was that all wore long trousers dragging on the ground; they had no other clothing except for cow and sheep hides. 
After Nanzhao was defeated, Jian Lang moved their tribe and settled them together with the Shi, Shun and other tribes, providing for their sustenance.

The He man were originally people from Xier He; they are now called the He man. 
In former times, when the six zhao states all existed, the He Man had already established and fortified their city at Dhe River. 
Before Kaiyuan, there had been tribal leaders who entered the court; local Cishi received rewards and returned. When Nanzhao Meng Guiyi captured a major city, the He man gradually moved forward to settle down, all being controlled by Langzhao. 
In the tenth year of Zhenyuan, after Langzhao was defeated and fell into decline, they moved again to live in Zhe Dong in northeastern Yunnan.

The Shi man were originally a branch of the Wu Man ethnic group. 
To the northwest of Tieqiao is Da Shi Ti, where Shi Qian and Lian Xun were among their inhabited areas. 
The "Nanzhao Zhuan" records that the Shi man resided in Da Shi Qian and Lianxun Qian, suggesting this passage may contain errors.

Men wore seamless trousers made of silk cloth; women divided their hair horizontally from the top, forming two buns at the forehead and the back of the head. 
Both men and women adorned themselves with necklaces and went barefoot, wearing sheepskin cloaks. 
The tribal leaders, in their submission to Tubo, were all falsely bestowed the title of Wang by Tubo. 
In the tenth year of Zhen Yuanyuan

The Shun man were originally a branch of Wu Man; initially, they lived together with the Shi man tribes in Jian and Gong Chuan. 
After Mieluo Pi and Duoluo Wang lost Leng Chuan and Langqiong, they retreated and seized Jian and Gong. As a result, they migrated to settle above Tie Qiao; the area is named Jian Qiang, located 200 li northwest of Lianxun Qian. The customs of men and women are roughly similar to those of the Shi man, and their tribal leaders were also granted the title of Wang by Tubo (Tibet). 
In the tenth year of Zhen Yuanyuan, Nanzhao Yi Mou Xun captured their king Bang Mi Qian and his clan, placing them in Baiyan of Yunnan to be supported and provided for. 
The common people of the Shi Man tribal group were scattered and assigned to various regions in the northeast.

磨蠻，亦烏蠻種類也。鐵橋上下及大婆、二婆、三探覽、昆池等川，皆其所居之地也。土多牛羊，一家即有羊群。終身不洗手面，男女皆披羊皮，俗好飲酒歌舞。此種本姚州部落百姓也。南詔既襲破鐵橋及昆池等諸城，凡虜獲萬戶，盡分隸昆川左右及西爨故地。
The Mo man were also a branch of the Wu man ethnic group. 
They lived in areas such as above and below Tieqiao, Dapo, Erpo, Santanlan, Kunchi, and other valleys. 
The land is rich in cattle and sheep; even a single household may have its own flock of sheep. 
They never wash their hands or faces for life, both men and women wear sheepskin cloaks; the custom is to enjoy drinking alcohol, singing, and dancing. 
This group was originally composed of common people from Ya Zhou tribal settlements. 
After Nanzhao captured Tieqiao and Kunchi and other cities, they took captive ten thousand households in total, who were all distributed to live on both sides of Kun Chuan and the former territory of Xi Cuan. 
13 
磨些蠻在施蠻外，與南詔為婚姻家，又與越析詔姻婭。

On the twenty-first day of March in the third year Xiantong, only about five or six thousand people set up camp at the southwest corner outside Annan Cheng. 
The rebel leader Yang Sijin entrusted the command of Luofu Zhou Fuxie Xian magistrate Ma Guanggao. 
Note: "Guanggao" was originally written as "Xiangao," but according to a reference in the Tongjian Kaoyi citing the Man Shu, which mentions Sijin and Guanggao's leadership, it is clear that "Xiangao" should be corrected to "Guanggao."
